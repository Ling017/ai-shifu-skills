# Input contract

Use existing context before asking questions.

For a definitive decision, identify source materials, whose experience they represent, and distinguishable themes/versions. Missing essentials permit provisional analysis or a focused question, not invented completeness.

Extract decision-changing context where available: buyer versus learner, desired outcome, target market, proof, format, duration, budget, exclusions, prior validation, and research restrictions. Explicit market wins over instruction language; business context is the next basis, with assumptions labeled. Ask when missing geography changes the decision.

Preserve source/version relationships. Separate facts, opinions, methods, cases, and outcome claims. Assign IDs such as SRC-001 with file/section or conversational locator. Extract relevant audience, problem, outcome, teachable core, proof, and boundary cues. Voice, keywords, and channels are conditional, not mandatory.

Missing proof does not permit invented authority. Missing audience evidence permits grounded hypotheses with no quota. Missing market/payment evidence means unverified and pending validation, not commercial GO. Material-only work may establish teaching sufficiency, not current market viability. Missing data does not prove absence of demand.
