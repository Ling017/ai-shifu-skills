# Decision model

Elaborate the six judgments in SKILL.md without imposing a fixed interview.

## Material and audience
Identify repeatable capability and unsupported claims. Map it to a learner blockage, distinguishing need, willingness to pay, and author credibility. For B2B, distinguish sponsor and learner.

## Market and product
Compare actual alternatives: courses, tools, free content, services, or doing nothing. Explain substantive teaching advantage or mark it unproven. Narrow the objective, change format, or reject the proposal when evidence does not support it. A new title alone is not differentiation.

## Teaching sufficiency
Judge whether coherent knowledge and teacher judgment, useful examples, practice/demonstration, and feedback criteria can accomplish the stated objective in the intended format. No lesson minimum applies: a focused session may suffice, while many disconnected lessons may not. A feasibility spine is not permission to author a course.

## Validation strength
Separate source support, public demand signals, inferred purchase motivations, and actual purchase/delivery/learning results. An advertised price, viral post, or provider's success claim is not verified conversion evidence. Explain contradictions and what would change the decision. Compare genuinely supported options without requiring backups.
