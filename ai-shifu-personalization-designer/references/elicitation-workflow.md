# Dynamic Elicitation Workflow

Use this workflow when the teacher's differentiated-teaching experience is still tacit, partial, or expressed mainly as learner labels.

## Elicitation Goal

First recover a reusable account of how this teacher helps people learn, including how the teacher changes approach. Then apply it to a course when a course is in scope. Do not complete a universal learner questionnaire or substitute a content outline for teaching expertise.

## Start From Existing Context

Before asking a question, extract and retain:

- teaching domains, audiences, relevant past contexts, and any existing teacher profile;
- course topic, objective, and fixed constraints only if a target course is already supplied;
- learner information already supplied;
- teacher examples, methods, language, and explicit non-negotiables;
- observed results versus expectations or opinions; and
- missing information that would actually change a teaching decision.

Ask only for information that materially improves the teacher profile or a course teaching decision. If cases are already supplied, synthesize first and ask about the most consequential uncertainty.

## Teacher Layer Before Course Layer

Start from experience, not “请填写你的教学风格”. Useful entry points include a typical teaching episode, a learner who needed a different approach, a method the teacher abandoned, or a moment the teacher decided to stop helping.

Explore whichever of these lenses the evidence makes useful; do not require all of them:

- **Learning priorities:** what counts as really learning, and what the teacher refuses to sacrifice.
- **Teaching sequence:** experience before concepts or concepts before practice; when and why that order changes.
- **Diagnosis:** what the teacher notices, possible causes, and how they distinguish similar-looking difficulties.
- **Support and learner agency:** when to demonstrate, give a partial hint, ask for an independent judgment, or withdraw help.
- **Explanation and interaction:** how examples, analogy, questions, silence, and language serve learning rather than merely decorate expression.
- **Feedback and motivation:** how the teacher corrects, challenges, reassures, or pauses without weakening the standard.

Recover enough of an episode to preserve the decision: “学员当时说了什么/做了什么 → 你怎么判断 → 你具体怎么带 → 为什么 → 学员如何回应 → 你接着怎么调整”. These are synthesis slots, not a six-question form. When useful, ask the teacher to reconstruct one short exchange; label recollection versus verbatim material.

Then summarize the teacher's characteristic method in plain language and ask about its transfer boundary: is it usual across teaching contexts, specific to this subject, or only appropriate under this condition? Do not demand several courses if the teacher has only one. Mark the available scope honestly, and allow the teacher to confirm a broader intended principle without inventing cross-course observations.

## Entering a Specific Course

After presenting or reusing the teacher profile, identify the course objective and learning bottlenecks. Select applicable patterns, translate them into course-specific teaching decisions, and explain how they would change explanation, questioning, support, practice, or feedback. A profile can remain partly provisional while the teacher reviews a proposed course application; unconfirmed parts do not become approved through reuse.

Background, level, goals, and preferences below help find relevant contrasts; do not let them replace the teacher-method inquiry.

## Information Lenses

These are optional search lenses, not a questionnaire. Select only the lenses relevant to the current teaching problem.

| Lens | Useful evidence | Teaching decisions it may change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Background and real context | role, field, prior lived or work experience, language or cultural context | examples, terminology, relevance framing, transfer task |
| Prior knowledge and skill | prerequisite knowledge, demonstrated ability, independence, stability of performance | prerequisite scaffolding, explanation depth, practice difficulty |
| Goal and success outcome | real task, assessment, desired deliverable, urgency, quality bar | emphasis, practice form, deliverable, verification standard |
| Current misconception and performance | answer, error pattern, blocked step, inability to transfer | correction path, boundary contrast, feedback, worked example |
| Preference, motivation, and self-efficacy | participation preference, interest, confidence, persistence | delivery style, example choice, feedback tone, participation route |
| Time, tools, resources, and accessibility | time budget, available software or materials, access need | minimum viable target, tool substitution, task size, presentation form |
| Learning response and change | response to feedback, repeated error, transfer, progress, regression | continue, deepen, remediate, revisit, or escalate support |

Do not assume demographic or identity information predicts ability, motivation, or learning style. Preference is weaker evidence than observed performance and may not be pedagogically appropriate to follow.

## Best Entry Prompts

Prefer one of these shapes, adapted to the teacher's language and existing information:

- “想一个具体内容：有没有两个学员学的是同一件事，但你明显用了不同的教法？”
- “有没有一次你发现常用讲法不奏效，于是临时换了方法？你先注意到了什么？”
- “面对哪种学员表现，你不会继续重复讲，而会换一种解释、练习或反馈？”
- “有没有一种差异看起来很明显，但你反而不会改变教学标准或方法？为什么？”

Use the teacher's own case before offering a hypothetical example. Offer a short example only when the teacher cannot get started.

## Dynamic Next-Question Logic

Ask one question per turn. Choose the gap with the highest value, not the first empty field:

1. **Only style adjectives or principles:** Ask for a teaching moment that shows what the teacher actually says or does.
2. **Only a learner label:** Ask what the teacher observes that justifies the label.
3. **Signal without teaching change:** Ask whether and how that information changes teaching. If it does not, exclude it from the design.
4. **Adjustment without trigger:** Ask when the teacher chooses that method and when they do not.
5. **Trigger and adjustment without rationale:** Ask what learning problem the change is meant to solve.
6. **No invariant:** Ask what knowledge, standard, boundary, or outcome remains unchanged.
7. **No evidence of effect:** Ask what learner behavior would show that the adjustment worked.
8. **No boundary or fallback:** Ask when the rule should not apply and what to do when information is missing.
9. **Complete episode but no reusable method:** Summarize the teacher's decision pattern and clarify where it transfers or changes.
10. **Only task procedure:** Ask how the teacher would help two learners who face the same task but need different support.
11. **Profile clear, course unclear:** Ask for the target learning outcome or bottleneck only if course application is requested.
12. **Profile or application ready:** Present the synthesis in plain language and invite scoped confirmation, correction, or rejection.

Do not continue through all lenses. Another case is useful when it changes the account of the teacher's method, tests transfer or a boundary, or resolves a conflict. Completing one local rule is not a substitute for synthesizing the requested teacher profile.

## Teacher-Experience Probes

When relevant, probe the teacher's expertise across these method families:

- diagnosis: signals, error patterns, and false signs of mastery;
- explanation: example, analogy, terminology, sequence, contrast, or mechanism;
- scaffolding: template, partial solution, checklist, demonstration, or staged hint;
- practice: imitation, correction, boundary judgment, authentic task, or transfer;
- feedback: direct correction, guided reflection, comparison, or confidence calibration;
- professional invariants: facts, quality standards, safety boundaries, and required outcomes;
- effectiveness judgment: recall, explanation, independent execution, transfer, or error reduction.

These probes help recover the teacher's method. They are not requirements to fill every category.

## Evidence Boundaries

Mark every input as one of:

- `learner_explicit`: explicitly stated by the learner;
- `teacher_observation`: directly observed by the teacher;
- `performance_evidence`: demonstrated in an answer or task;
- `teacher_experience_pattern`: a pattern generalized from prior cases;
- `ai_inference`: a provisional interpretation; or
- `unavailable`: not currently known.

An `ai_inference` may become a teaching hypothesis but never an observed learner fact. A prior teacher pattern may guide a candidate rule but still requires confirmation and a fallback for insufficient information.

## Stopping Conditions

Stop eliciting when:

- the requested teacher profile or course application is sufficiently concrete to guide design, its scope is clear, and additional inquiry would not materially change it;
- additional lenses would not change a teaching decision;
- the teacher chooses to stop or defer;
- the remaining question requires learner-time evidence not available during authoring; or
- the case is too weak, personal, or unsupported to abstract safely.

Do not equate case count with quality. One rich case can support a provisional or teacher-confirmed method, but cannot by itself prove stable behavior across courses. Stop with a useful bounded synthesis rather than inventing missing breadth.
